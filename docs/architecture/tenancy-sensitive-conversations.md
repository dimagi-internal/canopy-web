# Tenancy: sensitive conversations

What makes a conversation sensitive (`apps/harness/sensitivity.py`), who may mark and read it, and what it changes. Part of the [tenancy](tenancy.md) topic; the index there lists every part.

## Sensitive conversations (`apps/harness/sensitivity.py`)

`sensitivity.py::is_sensitive(session | turn)` is THE predicate: the requirements (`runner_requirements.requirements_of`, so a child, dispatched or forked turn inherits it) include `sensitive_data`, or cannot be read. Every surface below asks it, one test each in `tests/test_sensitive_storage.py`. The module docstring of `sensitivity.py` lists the surfaces; the bullets here are the rules a change must not break.

- **Stored less.** No `TurnTranscript` (`harness/ledger.py::append_transcript`). A tool result keeps only what `sensitivity.py::strip_result` allows (an allowlist, so a new field stays withheld); every stored event and `Message` keeps metadata only (`redact_payload`). The prompt is wiped at the turn's end (`harness/turn_close.py::close_turn`), the result note is withheld, and there is no owner-ask line, live tail (`RunnerBinding.tail`) or autotitle. A host's resource links are kept only from the site's own tool and only under schemes its signed `site.json` declares (`strip_result`, `host_grants/site_needs.py::is_site_tool`, `resource_schemes_for`); `tests/test_host_held_results.py` checks across every table that nothing else of a result survives.
- **The conversation is sealed to its own people.** canopy-web can WRITE a sensitive conversation and cannot read it back. The key pair, wraps and sealing format are in the module docstring of `apps/canopy_sessions/conversation_keys.py`; the byte format is pinned by `docs/vault/chat-v1/chat_v1_vectors.json` for both languages. What the code does not make obvious:
  - *The private half is never held by canopy-web.* A chat started in canopy's chat surfaces carries a key pair its starter's BROWSER made (`frontend/src/vault/sealedChat.ts::newConversationKey`, checked by `conversation_keys.py::check_offer`), kept even while the chat is ordinary, so if it turns sensitive its words are sealed to a key canopy-web never saw. **The limit:** a session made sensitive with no browser there (Slack, MCP, a contact, a host's grant, a chat that came with no key) gets a pair made on the server, its private half wrapped to the starter and dropped at once (`ensure_key`).
  - *Trust on first use is a decision.* A key never seen in this browser, or changed since it pinned one, is trusted on first use (`frontend/src/vault/trust.ts::trustSelfOnFirstUse`) and checked against the key the person actually holds the next time they open their vault key (`rememberSelf`). A key substituted by a database write, with no login or passkey, is DETECTED at that next unlock, not prevented, so a chat that turns private before then could be read by whoever holds that key. Refusing would stop every browser but one for anyone who replaced their key, with no way to tell the two apart.
  - *A fork, successor or branch carries the parent's key* only when its creator is one of the parent's own people (`conversation_keys.py::_carries_on`). Naming a private chat as `parent` gives anyone else a key of their own, none of its wraps, and none of its people (`canopy_sessions/services.py::_inherit_participants` asks the same).
  - *Each envelope is bound* to the conversation, its index and its speaker (`conversation_keys.py::message_info`). The browser opens a row only where its envelope says it belongs (`sealed_key.index_offsets` covers a fork's copied rows). No key canopy-web holds opens an envelope.
  - *ONE gate hands the ciphertext out:* `sensitivity.py::reads_content`, the conversation's own people (`is_conversation_person`: creator, participants) on its own chat surfaces (`request_is_chat_surface`, `scope_is_chat_surface`: a browser session or the widget's site token; never MCP, a PAT, OAuth or a session minted from a token). `readable` is the only function that returns it. Every other reader (search, human inputs, a session review, an agent, the fleet, an admin who is not in it) reads the metadata columns. Live frames to admitted sockets still carry the words in transit; only what is stored is unreadable to canopy-web.
  - *The reply reaches the asker the one way every turn's does:* its transcript rows. A confined turn has no binding, so its box tails the turn's own transcript while it runs and ships through the unbound route (`harness/session_sync.py::ingest_turn_end_rows`), then ships the whole transcript once more; every ship lands in the same epoch and a row already held is neither stored nor fanned out again.
  - *An agent's own login holds no vault key,* so it cannot start or read one, unless an admin of the agent turned on its TEST vault key, which receives copies only of the conversations that login is itself in (`vault.md` § An agent login's test vault key; `conversation_keys.py::add_wrap`).
  - *Who may send into one:* its own people on its chat surfaces, the read gate asked in `canopy_sessions/send_paths.py::send_from` for every send path (REST, socket, contact, app view, MCP, Slack): 403 `NOT_IN_IT_SEND`, `forbidden` on the socket. Whoever may not read the answer may not steer the agent. A leg that only lets someone see one (an agent's admins, a co-tenant of a runner-discovered one) gives VIEWER, never owner or editor (`access.py::role_for`). Nobody becomes the key's holder by sending: the key is its creator's (`conversation_keys.py::require_key`), so a conversation nobody started stays keyless and keeps no words.
  - *Who may hold one:* a canopy user with a vault key. A contact, or a starter with none, is refused (`CannotHold`: 422 on REST, `session.error` `cannot_hold` on the socket). A send that would MAKE an older conversation sensitive is refused before the switch, in its transaction (`add_runner_requirements(..., refuse_keyless=True)`): all or nothing, so a refused send never leaves it private with its history blanked. Rows an agent ships into a keyless sensitive conversation keep no words.
  - *Adding someone is a browser act by someone in it:* their browser unwraps its copy and wraps it to the newcomer's current vault key (`POST …/participants` with `wrap`; the server checks the shape and `recipient_jkt`). The key the server names for the newcomer is never taken on its word: the browser wraps only to a key it already pinned for that person or one the adder accepts now, having seen its fingerprint (`trust.ts::trustPerson`). A key that changed under a pinned person is a STOP until the adder types the fingerprint read by another channel (`PersonTrustPrompt.tsx::fingerprintMatches`). Removing someone deletes their wrap; the key is not rotated (they could have kept what they read). Nothing else makes anyone one of its people: `canopy_sessions/participants.py::may_join_implicitly` adds nobody who is not its creator and holds no wrap, so no entry point (a Slack reply, a dispatch's asker) can.
  - *Given it before it became sensitive:* a participant with no copy for their current vault key is `needs_key`; an editor or owner holding a current copy gives them one (`POST …/key-copies`). **A copy is written only where the person holds none for their CURRENT vault key** (`conversation_keys.py::add_wrap` → `CopyExists`, 409): the server cannot tell a good wrap from random bytes, so an overwrite could lock anyone out, the owner included. A stale copy is replaced; a current one that does not open is set aside by its holder alone (`set_aside_my_copy`). Until they hold a copy the live socket turns them away (`sensitivity.py::hears_live`). Creating a new vault key re-wraps every copy the person can open (`PUT /my-keys`, `frontend/src/vault/conversationCopies.ts`); one not carried is refreshed by an editor.
  - *Rows from before it became sensitive* are sealed in the transaction that makes it sensitive (`services.py::add_runner_requirements` → `conversation_keys.py::seal_existing`): the server holds the PUBLIC key, so it seals what it can no longer read back, clears the columns and drops a tool's result. An ended turn's prompt is wiped, its note withheld, events reduced and raw transcript deleted, and the binding's tail, summary and dialog words cleared (`services.py::_withhold_what_it_kept`). A live turn keeps its prompt until it ends. "Its turns" are every turn whose conversation it is (`sensitivity.py::conversation_of`, `turns_of_q`). Those turn rows are locked by the switch and `ledger.append_events` reads sensitivity under the same lock, so an event written as it switches is never kept in clear; `drafts.update_draft` asks again under the session row's lock. Every attached file is deleted in the same transaction (`services.py::_drop_attachments`): sealing it needs a browser holding the key, which a send the server handles has not got. A FORK made while it was ordinary holds a copy of the same words, so it becomes sensitive with it, recursively; any other child keeps its own state.
  - *The keyset and the host.* `sealed_key.keyset` (the reader's vault public key and its seals) goes only to the person's browser: canopy's own page or the panel's frame, whose fetches are `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin` (`sensitivity.py::request_is_panel_browser`). A site's backend calling with the same delegated token gets `keyset: null`. This is not a proof against a site that forges the header; it keeps the seals out of every ordinary server-to-server call (a passkey seal cannot be attacked offline, a passphrase one can). **Trust boundary:** a host site vouches for who its visitor is, so it can act as them and read what canopy hands them (ciphertext and their wrap, never anything that opens it). **A host site's backend hears live words:** it holds the visitor's delegated token, so it can open the chat socket or send as them. Sealing protects what canopy-web STORES, not a site the person chose to use canopy through.
  - *Drafts stay in the browser.* A sensitive conversation stores no draft: `drafts.py::update_draft` returns None, and becoming sensitive empties every draft (`forget_words`).
  - *Files: the agent's copy for one turn, the people's sealed* (`canopy_sessions/private_attachments.py` module docstring). The agent reads a file on a box that holds no key, so a file is treated like the prompt: uploaded in clear for one turn (only from its people's browser: `api_attachments.py::_from_its_peoples_browser`, a token, MCP, a minted session, a contact or a site's backend gets 422), then the name, type, size and bytes are sealed to the conversation's public key by that browser (`frontend/src/vault/sealedFiles.ts`). A send claims only a file whose sealed copy is stored (`send.py::claim_pending_attachments`). The runner's download serves it ONCE, to the box holding that turn, deleting object, name and key as it does (`take_for_runner`); the turn's end (`close_turn` → `forget_turn`) and the sensitive sweep delete any copy not fetched. The plaintext content route serves it to nobody. On the box the file lands in the turn's own owner-only directory, readable by that turn alone (`runner/ec2/cloud_runner.py::sensitive_data_settings`, `attachment_dir`) and removed when the turn ends (`_sensitive_data_forget_turn`).
- **Memory is off, both ways:** nothing is written (`contacts/hcp/sensitive.py::refuse_if_sensitive`) and nothing is read into one (`contacts/hcp/consent.py::memory_state` reads every feature as not effective in a sensitive session; `hcp.effective` / `hcp.require` refuse a sensitive turn, so `hcp_searchPreferences` is a 403). The person cannot switch it on there (`tests/test_hcp.py`).
- **Live and log routes ask the same gate:** the chat socket (`SessionConsumer`) admits only `reads_content`; the turn socket's tail gets stored metadata; `harness/turn_access.py::can_read_turn_content` is False for a sensitive turn, so no turn read (REST, MCP, the board, the unclaimable report, the caller envelope) returns its prompt, note or report while it runs. A sensitive turn no runner has started is closed MISSED through `close_turn`, prompt wiped, after `CANOPY_SENSITIVE_TURN_DEADLINE_SECONDS` (`harness/sweeps.py::expire_unrun_sensitive_turns`; never while another turn of its conversation is running). Session info shows an allowlist of metadata (`api_read.py::INFO_METADATA_KEYS`) and for a sensitive one no box Claude session or summary.
- **Only its people hand it on.** Adding a participant and forking need `reads_content`, so an agent's admin who may open a thread they are not in cannot add themselves or copy it. A fork is also the OWNER's act (`access.py::can_share`): it carries the key, the wraps and the agent's memory into a conversation its forker owns. `metadata.fork` is server-owned (create takes only `services.py::HOST_METADATA_KEYS` and `x_…`), and what the runner is told (`placement.py::fork_plan`) is read from canopy's own columns: `worktree_of` only within the fork's lineage (`fork_lineage`, which the cloud runner checks), the cut only from the recorded `parent_claude_session`. No member can aim their turn at someone else's worktree.
- **Nothing from outside its people:** Slack takes no message into one, even from its owner, and says so (`slack/services.py::private_refusal`); an email in its thread runs as the agent's own turn beside it (`harness/channels/email.py::arrive`). It is not moved from Slack either (`slack/services.py::_move_to_cloud`), since its stranded turn could only be re-asked by Slack sending into it.
- **The agent's memory (kept conversations):** a turn of a sensitive conversation resumes the previous turn's Claude session only on the same sensitive-data box, host site and agent, while the box still holds the transcript. The box records which session belongs to which conversation (`runner/ec2/cloud_runner.py::kept_resume`, `keep_conversation`), told by the claim (`ClaimedTurnOut.sensitive_conversation`), keeps only that session's transcript until the conversation has been idle for the box's keep window or is closed, and forgets all of them when the box asks `POST /runners/{id}/kept-conversations` and gets a 401, 403 or 404. Otherwise the turn starts fresh and is told so (`conversation.starts_fresh` in the envelope): canopy-web never reads the sealed words back to hand them on. No turn's read tools reach another conversation's kept words: they are scoped to the turn's own directory and envelope, and every transcript, the kept-conversation records and other per-turn state under `~/.canopy` are denied by name (`sensitive_data_settings`). A member's `who_is_asking` is gated like its REST twin (`can_read_turn_content`).
- **No route around the gate** (`tests/test_sensitive_read_routes.py`, `tests/test_sealed_server_routes.py`): a session minted from a token is refused by the app-view routes (`app_views_api.py::browser_only`), the panel's own token (`embedding/embed_api.py::embed_self_token`) and the chat socket. Every page route (state and actions, declared, listed, invoked, answered; MCP `read_page_state`, `list_page_actions`, `invoke_page_action`) asks `reads_content` (`api_page.py::_page_in_it_or_403`); `current_page` and the `page_*` tools leave a sensitive one out; an action's arguments and answer are not kept (`page_actions.py::_forget`); the page is forgotten when its last live turn ends (`turn_close.py::_forget_the_page`) or after `CANOPY_SENSITIVE_PAGE_TTL_SECONDS` with no live turn (`page_state.py::forget_idle_sensitive_pages`). The unbound stream route takes only the confined turn's own transcript from a box that may still hold the agent (`api_session_sync.py::_turn_end_session`, `agents/holding.py::runner_may_hold_agent`). A list reads who is in which once per page (`sensitivity.py::people_in`); a View's `tool_input` asks the request's real surface; attachment bytes ask `reads_content` (404 otherwise).
- **A blocked agent's dialog is metadata:** `RunnerBinding.pending_question` keeps source, stamp, numbered options and a digest, never the words (`sensitivity.py::menu_metadata`); it is not relayed to Slack and its push says nothing of the question.
- **The socket re-asks:** the `access.recheck` message (`consumers.py::SessionConsumer.access_recheck`) asks the ACL and `hears_live` again. It is sent to every socket on a session that becomes sensitive (`services.py::add_runner_requirements`) and to a removed participant's (`services.py::recheck_sockets`).
- **No title:** create drops one, becoming sensitive clears one, `PUT …/title` is 422 (`sensitivity.py::NO_TITLE`), and the `SESSION_CREATED` line keeps none.
- **Who a site may say a visitor is takes a passkey:** a signed `email_verified` claim resolves to a member, whose token reads their sensitive chats, so registering a host site or changing its public keys, JWKS URL or issuer needs a fresh step-up (`embedding/host_sites_api.py`).
- **MCP Apps:** which View a call has is decided from metadata columns alone (`sensitivity.py::call_of`); a View gets `tool_result: null` and fetches its data as the viewer from its input, which the server sends sealed (`sealed_call_for`) and the browser decrypts; a receipt keeps no excerpt; `ui/update-model-context` is accepted and not kept (`mcp_apps_host/mcp_apps_views.py::withheld_result`).
- **Never re-exposed:** share-link upload, Slack share / new thread and mirroring (`slack.relay`), export are refused (`sensitivity.py::REFUSAL`). **Never re-processed:** auto-debug starts nothing for a turn with any requirement (`harness/auto_debug.py`); the fleet's transcript readers find none to read.
- **A sensitive turn's token reaches a fixed few tools:** the host site's globs ∩ `agents/sensitive_data.py::SENSITIVE_CANOPY_TOOLS`, plus its own page's actions (`sensitive_data.sensitive_tool`: each runs in its person's tab and answers only the turn). It resolves to nothing once the host site stops saying `data: sensitive` (re-read on every call) or the turn is not confined (`harness/caller_tokens.py::_sensitive_grant`, `mcp/turn_scope.py`). So a sensitive turn creates no schedule, task or turn of its own.
- **Lineage is never dropped:** a caller token's own turn is the parent of anything its request starts, whatever a header or payload names (`common/request_context.py::from_request`, `harness/provenance.py::requested_parent`); a payload `parent` that names nothing is a 422 (`UnknownParentError`). A session started from a sensitive turn or conversation takes its requirements at birth (`provenance.py::inherit_requirements`); a turn started from one makes the conversation it joins sensitive (`sensitivity.py::take_on`, at enqueue). A queued turn whose recorded parent has since been deleted is unsatisfiable (`runner_requirements.py::lost_lineage`).
- **One sealing decision:** every writer of `Message` rows (`send.py`, `transcript_ingest`) asks `sensitivity.py::seals(session, turn)` on the locked session, which first raises it to the turn's requirements, so what is sealed and what `reads_content` admits agree.
- **Elsewhere:** a turn refused at creation keeps no prompt (`harness/services.py::_apply_capability`); forced retention reaches a session-less turn sensitive through any ancestor (`retention/services.py::_sweep_sensitive`, `tenancy-session-access-and-retention.md` § Content retention). Tests: `tests/test_sensitive_lineage.py`.
