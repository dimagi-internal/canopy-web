# Workspace email domains (`apps/mail`)

A workspace admin brings their own domain; canopy-web runs its email through Amazon SES. **Agent addresses** (`ada@domain`) turn incoming mail into the agent's email turn and send as that address. **Every other address** is forwarded to a canopy user's login email (a catch-all route catches the rest). Setup is Settings → Email (`/w/:workspace/settings/email`); the operator steps are `docs/runbooks/mail-domain-onboarding.md`. A superuser can **share** a domain with other workspaces (below), so one domain serves as a sitewide address space instead of every workspace bringing its own. A domain's `agent_addresses` switch gives every agent that may use it a real `<slug>@<domain>` route (`apps/mail/auto_addresses.py`).

Not here: IMAP, composing HTML, custom folders or labels, shared inboxes, replying to forwarded mail *as* the domain, automatic DNS writes, bulk mail. A forward route can keep a copy of each message as a thread in its destination's private inbox (`/w/:workspace/mail`); every inbound message, dropped ones included, is recorded in a metadata-only ledger (`MailInboundLog`) admins read in Settings → Email. A person composes only from their own forwarding addresses, never as another person's address or the catch-all (`apps/mail/api.py`, the `from_address` checks).

## Tier and removability

`apps/mail/` is a **product-tier** app: framework apps never import it (`tests/test_architecture_boundary.py`); the framework reaches it through `apps/agents/mailbox.py` and registered hooks (`apps/mail/agent_move.py`). It does not extend `apps/inbound` — `InboundMailbox` is Gmail-push routing and stays Gmail-only; SES routes are `MailRoute`, so `/runner-mailboxes` never lists them and no runner polls them with `gog`. Gate: the `perms.INTEGRATIONS` capability for domains and routes, `AGENT_WORK` (plus FULL access from `turn_context.for_asker` on the agent, `apps/mail/api.py`) to send, reply or change a thread's state, and workspace membership to view threads.

## Model

Fields and their meaning are on the models (`apps/mail/models.py`); settings and defaults are the `MAIL_*` block in `config/settings/base.py`. What the code does not make obvious:

- **A domain's routing is inert until `verified`** (`MailDomain.routes_live`): DKIM verifying is the proof of DNS control. `name` is globally unique because SES identities are account-wide; a row still `pending` past `MAIL_PENDING_DOMAIN_TTL_DAYS` no longer holds it.
- **Route resolution:** exact local part, then `*`, then drop (`domains.resolve`). The catch-all can forward or drop, never belong to an agent (422); `canopy-forwarder` (the From of forwarded mail) cannot be routed; `dmarc` (the `rua` address of the DMARC record) is never taken by the catch-all, so DMARC reports are dropped unless `dmarc@` has its own route; the role addresses are only the domain's own workspace's to route (`domains._OWNER_ONLY`). An agent may hold several routes; a reply always goes out from the thread's own route.
- **Forward destinations are `User` FKs** (a workspace member), never typed addresses (`MailRoute.forward_to`).
- **Removing a route or a domain keeps every thread and message.** A thread's `route` is `SET_NULL`; reads use the snapshotted `address` and `agent_slug`; a reply on a thread whose route is gone or disabled is 409 "this address no longer exists".
- **Threading** is by reply headers scoped to one route (`apps/mail/threads.py`); messages dedupe on `message_id` per workspace + mailbox.
- **A thread's folder is derived, never stored** (`api.py::_in_folder`): Inbox, Sent, Archived, Spam (`spam` flag) and Trash (`trashed_at`). A list that names no `folder` is live threads at its `archived` state, so an agent's tools never read spam or trash. Trash is deleted for good after `storage.TRASH_DAYS` by the daily tick (`storage.purge_expired`), or at once by `DELETE …/threads/{ws}/{uuid}` (only from Trash or Spam, 409 otherwise) or `POST …/empty-folder/{ws}`. `storage.delete_threads` keeps a `kept/` raw another mailbox's copy cites.
- **`MailMessage.bcc` is outbound only:** on the SES envelope, never in the headers, and counted against both daily caps and `MAX_RECIPIENTS`.
- **Ledgers hold no body:** `MailInboundLog` is written by `inbound.record`, which never raises, so a ledger fault cannot change delivery; `MailForwardLog` and `MailEvent` are metadata only, and `MailEvent` is unique on the SNS `MessageId` so a redelivery counts once.

## Mailbox cap

Canopy hosts agent mail only so an agent has a working mailbox, so each one is small: `MAIL_MAILBOX_CAP_BYTES` per (workspace, `MailMessage.mailbox`) address, inbound and outbound copies alike, and `MAIL_MAX_ATTACHMENT_BYTES` of attachment content kept per inbound message. `storage.enforce_cap` (module docstring) runs after each delivery (`agent_mail.deliver`) and send (`agent_mail.send`, outside the domain lock): it evicts the oldest mail (Trash and Spam, then archived, then read, then unread) and **never the message just stored**, even if it alone is over. A failing eviction is logged and never fails the delivery or send. An attachment that does not fit is still a `MailAttachment` row with `kept = False` and no bytes in S3, and a message that dropped one keeps no `kept/` raw copy (`raw_key = ""`), because the raw MIME holds every attachment. `GET /api/mail/agents/{slug}/mailbox` reports `used_bytes` and `cap_bytes`.

## Shared domains

One domain, many workspaces (`MailDomainShare`). The domain's own workspace keeps everything domain-wide: DNS records, Re-check, Remove, the catch-all, the role addresses, the domain-wide `daily_send_cap` and lifting a suspension. A workspace the domain is shared with only adds routes for **its own** agents and members.

- **Who:** sharing is `PUT/DELETE /api/mail/domains/{workspace}/{id}/shares`, `superuser` only (not a workspace setting: it lets one tenant hold addresses on another tenant's domain). A grant covers the named workspace and every workspace below it (nearest grant wins, `domains.share_for`).
- **Addresses are first come, first served** across every workspace on the domain: a local part another workspace holds is 409 "that address is already taken", never replaced and never naming the holder.
- **Each workspace sees only its own addresses**, the owner included (`_domain_out` filters routes by workspace); threads, messages, attachments and forwards belong to the route's workspace.
- **Two caps on a send:** the domain-wide cap counts every workspace's sends; a shared workspace also has its grant's `daily_send_cap`, counted for that workspace alone. A bounce or complaint anywhere counts toward the one domain's reputation and a suspension stops every workspace on it: that is the cost of sharing, and why the per-workspace cap exists.
- **Revoking** a grant deletes no route, thread or message: the routes go inert (`domains.route_is_live`: inbound falls through to the owner's catch-all, a send is 409 "no longer shared") and re-sharing revives them.

## AWS side (`deploy/aws/canopy-mail.cfn.yaml`, stack `canopy-web-mail`, labs account, us-east-1)

Deployed by hand with a labs admin login (the deploy role holds no `ses:*`), from `main` after merge. The stack's header comment and the template say what each resource is; `tests/test_mail_stack.py` pins the invariants. What to know:

- The inbound bucket is `DeletionPolicy`/`UpdateReplacePolicy: Retain`, because `kept/` is the only raw copy of agent mail. SES writes `incoming/` (expires after 7 days); agent mail is copied to `kept/` (`ses.py`, no expiry).
- Receipt rule set `canopy-inbound` has ONE rule, `canopy-all` (no recipient filter, spam/virus scan on, TLS required), notifying SNS → `POST /api/mail/ses/inbound/` with the object key only. It must be activated by hand (`aws ses set-active-receipt-rule-set`; that deactivates any other active set). Configuration set `canopy-tenant-mail` (every tenant send) publishes send/delivery/bounce/complaint/reject to SNS → `POST /api/mail/ses/events/`. Both subscriptions carry a `DeliveryPolicy` of about 40 minutes of retries.
- The policy on canopy-web's task role (`TaskRoleName`) creates/inspects/deletes identities, reads and writes the bucket, deletes only `kept/` and `attachments/` objects (the mailbox cap), and sends with a `ses:FromAddress` condition barring Dimagi addresses. That condition limits only this policy (IAM allows are additive and `canopy-ses-send` grants unconditioned sends), so it is defence in depth; the real guard is app-level (below).
- Settings come from the stack Outputs via `canopy-web.cfn.yaml` parameters (`MAIL_ENABLED`, `MAIL_INBOUND_BUCKET`, `MAIL_INBOUND_TOPIC_ARN`, `MAIL_EVENTS_TOPIC_ARN`); the rest are the `MAIL_*` block in `config/settings/base.py`. With `MAIL_ENABLED` off, adding a domain and send/reply are 503 and inbound is acknowledged as `dropped:mail-off`.

## Flows

- **Add a domain** (`domains.py`): a holder still `pending` past the TTL, in any workspace, is released under a row lock: SES is asked whether its identity verified (if so the row flips to `verified` and the add is 409); otherwise the row, its routes and then its SES identity are deleted. Any provider error other than NotFound keeps the row (502). The release is folded into the adder's audit row without naming the holder's workspace. A fresh pending, verified or suspended holder is 409.
- **Verification:** status flips to `verified` when SES reports DKIM success, refreshed lazily on every domain read and by `manage.py mail_refresh`. **SES gives up after 72 hours** if it does not find the DKIM CNAMEs (or the `bounce.` MX/SPF): its status turns `FAILED` and it never looks again on its own. A refresh then stores the reason as `verification_error` (the domain stays `pending`). Only the explicit Re-check (`POST …/refresh`, never the lazy refresh or `mail_refresh`) restarts it, and only for what SES failed whose records now check out in live DNS (`domains.refresh(restart=True)`, at most once per 10 minutes per domain). If SES answers the DKIM restart with different tokens the row takes them and `restart_note` tells the admin to publish them.
- **Inbound** (`inbound.py` docstring has the retry discipline): the SNS signature is verified **before anything else is parsed** (`sns.py`: cert and subscribe URLs must be `https://sns.<region>.amazonaws.com`, the topic ARN ours). A 4xx fetching the cert is a bad message (404); a network error, timeout or 5xx is transient (500, SNS retries) so a blip never drops real mail. Unknown or unverified domain, or a virus FAIL verdict, is dropped and logged, never forwarded and never wakes an agent. A spam FAIL is never forwarded and never wakes an agent either; a mailbox that stores mail (an agent's, or a forward route that keeps a copy) holds it in its Spam folder (`inbound._spam`), anywhere else it is `dropped:spam`, and any problem storing spam is a drop, never a redelivery. Only a transient failure answers 500; any other error is acknowledged 200 as `error:<Type>` so a poisoned message cannot loop; per-recipient dedupe makes a redelivery safe. **Forward:** re-sent through SES from `"<Name> via <domain>" <canopy-forwarder@domain>` with `Reply-To` the original sender; a destination on any canopy mail domain is dropped as `dropped:loop`.
- **Replay:** `manage.py mail_replay <incoming-key> [--recipient ADDR …] [--dry-run] [--reforward]` re-handles a raw object still in `incoming/` through the same code as a notification (`inbound.plan`/`deliver`). Keys outside `incoming/` or containing `..` are refused; a spam or virus FAIL verdict still drops; a forward logged as dropped is retried only with `--reforward`; dedupe makes repeating it safe.
- **Agent turn** (`agent_mail.py`): `harness.services.enqueue_turn` with origin `email`, prompt `/<agent>:turn --thread <uuid>`, `origin_ref.backend = "ses"` and `origin_ref.mailbox` = the route address it arrived at. A refused turn's automatic sender notice on an SES thread is sent from the thread's route (`agents.mailbox.reply` → `agent_mail.send`), never through Gmail. Stored without a turn: the agent's own address as sender, and automated mail (`parse.py`: `Auto-Submitted` ≠ `no`, `Precedence: bulk|list|junk`, `List-Unsubscribe`). Sender grading uses the server-only `authserv_id` (`MAIL_SES_AUTHSERV_ID`); runner-posted Gmail turns keep `INBOUND_EMAIL_AUTHSERV_ID`, so a runner can never choose its own trusted receiver. No runner reads the mailbox.
- **Who sees the thread's session:** the members on the thread (From/To/Cc of each aligned inbound message and the To/Cc of the agent's own sends) become its participants as each message arrives, and viewers of the work session that message ran in (`access-sessions-and-people.md` § Who is in a session).
- **The thread's session retires on its own** (`harness/mail_anchor.py`): it is an ANCHOR (the runner runs each turn in its own work session, keyed on the thread id), archived once every turn on it and every work session they handed off to is terminal or archived; new mail revives it under the same row lock. By hand: `manage.py retire_mail_anchors [--dry-run]`. An anchor is never listed; the WORK session is the row a person finds. The session is found-or-created under the agent's row lock, so push and poll discovering one message at once make one session.
- **One turn per thread at a time, for trigger mail** (`harness/mail_coalesce.py`): mail that fires an `inbound_mail` trigger folds into the thread's QUEUED turn; while a turn RUNS one follow-up queues; after a turn that ran finishes, further mail is held (`PendingMail`) for the trigger's `mail_quiet_minutes` and then starts ONE turn carrying it all (`sweeps.release_held_mail`). Mail from a person (no trigger) is never coalesced.
- **Reputation guard** (`reputation.py`): every event is recorded (sends are the denominator); a Bounce, Complaint or Reject triggers an evaluation, and so does the Send that brings the window to the minimum volume. The sending domain is found by an exact match on the event's `mail.source`; an event matching no `MailDomain` is ignored. A suspended domain refuses sends but still stores and forwards inbound; an admin re-enables. The thresholds sit under SES's own review lines so a domain is stopped here before AWS pauses sending for connect-labs too. `agent_mail.send` holds a `select_for_update` lock on the domain row across the status/cap check, the SES call and the insert, so concurrent sends cannot overshoot `daily_send_cap` (no message row is written if SES refuses); sends use their own fail-fast SES client (`ses.py`) because the lock is held across the call.

## Safety invariants

Forward destinations are User FKs only — no free-text destination exists, so the feature cannot be a relay. Tenant sends go through `canopy-tenant-mail` (always set by `ses.send_raw`) and `agent_mail.send`/`forward` only ever use a `MailDomain` address; the IAM condition on Dimagi addresses is a backstop for its own policy only. HTML renders (`frontend/src/components/mail/MessageView.tsx`) in an iframe sandboxed with `allow-popups allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox` only (never scripts, forms or same-origin) behind a no-network CSP, on a forced white page, with any `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` and `<base>` stripped first and every link rewritten to a new tab, or its `href` removed unless it is http, https or mailto (CSP does not stop navigation, and an escaping popup must never carry a script URL). "Show images" lifts the CSP for `https:` images on one message, on the reader's click, and is not offered in Spam; attachments download via short-lived presigned URLs handed only to workspace members reading the thread. Only the mailbox cap, and a delete from Trash or Spam (the person's own, or FULL on the agent), ever delete a thread. Every route declares its `@gate` (domain/route writes are `setting=True`, audited via `audit.note_change`; send, reply, inbound and events name their kind).

## An agent's addresses and its Google identity

An agent's addresses and its Google login are separate facts. `Agent.email` is its own (primary) address (a Gmail mailbox or a canopy-hosted one); its other addresses are its agent routes and its Gmail push mailboxes (`domains.agent_addresses`, served as `addresses` on the agent's mailbox). `Agent.google_account` is the Google account it signs in to Drive, Docs and gog with, and `Agent.google_identity()` resolves it (`apps/agents/models/agent.py`) — so an agent with only `<slug>@dimagi.ai` sets `google_account` to `<slug>@dimagi-ai.com`. Framework code reaches all of this through `apps/agents/mailbox.py` (`describe`, `addresses`, `reply`), never by importing this app.
