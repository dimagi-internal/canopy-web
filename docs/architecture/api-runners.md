# API: runners and capabilities

The rules the runner-registry and capability-lease routes enforce that their schemas do not show: who may call, what only a browser may do, what is anonymous and why, and the invariants between routes. Part of the [api surface](api-surface.md) topic; the index there lists every part. The routes themselves (methods, paths, bodies, status codes) are in `/api/openapi.json`, whose descriptions are the route docstrings.

Module docstrings in `api_runner_pairings.py`, `machine_pairing.py`, `api_runner_sessions.py` and `api_device_key_requests.py` carry the pairing flows.

## Who may act on a runner (three tiers, all 404 rather than 403)
- **See** (`apps/harness/runner_queries.py::runner_read_q`): runners homed in, or serving (`routing.runner_tenant_slugs`, the claim gate), the caller's tenants, whoever paired them, so a turn can be pinned to any box claim would give it. A runner with no workspace is visible only to its owner. A computer's key holder (`Runner.COMPUTER`) is never listed.
- **Act as** (`runner_queries.py::runner_or_404`, route gate `runner`): the runner's owner alone, and everything that speaks FOR the box: heartbeat, claim, reports, retire/unretire, pause/unpause, granting or revoking runner admins, making it private. A runner nobody owns is acted on by nobody. Do not bind this to a specific token: tokens rotate; ownership is the boundary.
- **Administer** (`apps/harness/api.py::runner_admin_or_404` -> `runners.py::can_administer_runner`, gate `runner-admin`): the owner or a `RunnerAdmin`; credentials, browser sign-in, device keys, enrollments, flags, egress, engine, name, refresh, repo adds. Only the owner grants admin, so the grant cannot propagate.
- `RunnerOut.can_manage` / `can_administer` tell each caller which listed rows it may act on / administer. Retired runners 404 everywhere except `unretire` (`include_retired`).
- A **private** runner: only its owner may register or approve a device key on it (`runners.py::may_trust_device_keys`, 403); admins still see its keys and may revoke. Only the owner makes a runner private.
- A runner is owned by a person: pairing, invitations, pairing approval and fleets refuse an agent's login (403); grant the agent admin instead (`api_runners.py::pair_runner`).

## Browser-only and step-up
A token cannot answer these (plain 403); a browser gets 403 `step_up_required` until it confirms a passkey (`apps/passkeys/stepup.py::require_recent_step_up`):
- Approving a device key (`api_device_keys.py::approve_runner_device_key`, session only), a cloud invitation (`api_runner_invitations.py::approve_runner_invitation`), a laptop pairing (`api_runner_pairings.py::approve_runner_pairing`); creating a fleet is approved on the same invitation page.
- Declaring runner flags (`api_runners_settings.py::set_runner_flags`), and adding an allowed host to a sensitive-data box's egress (`set_runner_egress`: a sensitive-data box cannot be unlocked, 422; tightening it needs no step-up).
Different rule, do not conflate: credential-set writes (`api_runner_credential_sets.py`) and capability slot data (`apps/capabilities/api.py::set_capability_slot_data`) need a step-up only when the caller is a browser; a token passes.

## Anonymous routes: the proof is the authority
These are `auth=None` and public in `apps/common/middleware.py`. Every refusal is the one uniform 401 so a caller learns nothing about which runners, keys, invitations or roles exist; the reason goes to the log.
- `POST /api/runner-sessions/` (`api_runner_sessions.py`): an `X-Canopy-Aws-Proof` or `DPoP` proof for this URL from an ACTIVE device key of the named runner buys a `RunnerAccessToken` (an hour; authenticates as the runner's owner). `runner_auth.py::reaches` is the allowlist of what it may call, by path (the runner protocol and the reads a box makes to prepare agents; a private runner adds `local-turns`; a computer gets less); everything else is 403, so a box cannot read workspace content or change a setting as its owner. It stops working when the key is revoked, the runner retired or the owner deactivated (`RunnerAccessToken.lookup`).
- `POST /api/runner-device-key-requests/` (`api_device_key_requests.py`): a box whose PAT was revoked (within 30 days, never another person's) proves that token plus the key it registers and gets a PENDING key only, at most 3 pending per runner (429); nothing accepts it until a person approves in a browser.
- `POST /api/harness/runners/pair-by-role` (`api_runner_invitations.py::pair_runner_by_role`, `machine_pairing.py`): the instance-role proof is the identity and an invitation its creator APPROVED is the authority. The creator must still hold `agent.work` in the workspace at pairing. A role that belongs to a fleet joins through `fleets.join` instead. A replaced instance re-attaches (same role and name); for a machine of several runners the new key replaces the old in place, keeping runner id, bindings and vault wraps.
- `POST /api/harness/runner-pairings/` and `…/{id}/claim` (`api_runner_pairings.py`): DPoP by the pairing's key. The approve step hands the one-time secret only to the box's loopback `redirect_url` (stored as sha256), so whoever approves must be at the machine. Claim re-checks that the approver is active and still holds `agent.work` in the chosen workspace. A key canopy already knows as pending, revoked or a retired runner's is refused, never silently re-paired.
- `POST /api/harness/runners/box-egress` (`api_box_egress.py`): root on a cloud box proves its instance role and reads the effective egress lock of every runner on that machine.

## Invitations and fleets
`runner-invitations` (`api_runner_invitations.py`): the creator needs `agent.work` in the workspace (non-member 404); `GET /check` runs the same gates without creating anything (`up.sh` asks before any AWS write). Someone else's live invitation for the same role and name is never replaced (409 naming nobody), only your own or an expired one; creating one for a fleet's role is 422. Only the creator reads or approves one (else 404). Fleet routes are owner-only (`gate("self")`, else 404): [`runner-fleets.md`](runner-fleets.md).

## Heartbeat and capabilities: reported, never declared
- `HeartbeatIn` (`apps/harness/schemas/runners.py`): **absent is not empty**. An omitted `projects`, `health`, `mailboxes_readable` or `clis` leaves the stored value alone; `projects: []` means "none". A runner that cannot read emdash must omit `projects`. Provenance fields (`code_*`) are assigned unconditionally.
- `data_mode` is believed only from a CLOUD runner's beat signed with its own device key (`runner_protocol.py::data_mode_trusted`); anything else is recorded as none, because a sensitive-data declaration relies on a report an admin cannot type.
- `PATCH /api/harness/runners/{id}` (`api_runners_fleet.py::update_runner_capabilities`) replaces `capabilities` but 422s on a hand-written change to any runner-reported key (`projects`, `profiles`, `envelope`, `midturn`, `claude_model`, `clis`, …; an unchanged echo is dropped), preserves those across a write that omits them, believes `jobs` only from the cloud box itself, and never lets a write replace or clear the server-owned `serves`. `capabilities` is a routing hint, never a boundary; a reported confinement level is a security property.
- Pairing (`POST /api/harness/runners/`) drops reported keys from `capabilities`, refuses an agent login (403), needs `workspace` when the caller has no single default (422), and enrolls the box only in the workspace it paired into.

## Enrollment and lifecycle
- Enrollment (`api_runners_access.py`, `runners.py::enroll_runner`): a box serves a workspace only when enrolled there (or in one above it) AND its OWNER holds `agent.work` there. Enrolling is refused (422) when the owner is not an editor there, for a `COMPUTER`, and when a cloud runner would span two workspace trees; a caller who is not a member of the workspace gets 404. An administrator sees only the enrollments in workspaces they belong to. Unenrolling does nothing effective while a workspace above is still enrolled; a held turn runs to completion.
- `retire` removes the runner from every `RunnerAssignment` and `WorkspaceRunnerOrder` and cancels queued turns pinned to it; `unretire` restores `DISCONNECTED` and not those routes (`api_runners_lifecycle.py`). Prefer `pause`: server-enforced (`live_status` = `PAUSED`), outranks a pin, stops starting work never finishing it; `until` never shortens a scheduled unpause or attaches one to an open-ended pause.
- Holds: fleet hold needs a superuser or a named holder (`CANOPY_FLEET_HOLDERS`), release a superuser; workspace hold/stop its owner or superuser (`api_runners_holds.py`); per-agent hold and release need `agent.work` (`apps/agents/api.py::hold_agent`). A hold stops STARTING turns; they keep enqueuing and wait QUEUED, running turns finish.
- `refresh` (`api_runners_protocol.py::refresh_runner`, `runner_refresh.py`, `runners.py::request_refresh`) is administer-tier and durable; it is discharged only by the box reporting a bootstrap newer than the request, never by guessing; a request already pending is left as it is.
- `egress` (`egress.py`): a sensitive-data box (`runner_requirements.is_sensitive_data_box`) is always locked; the box never loosens below its own floor.
- Credential sets (`api_runner_credential_sets.py`): only the runner's owner shares or chooses a set; a fleet member, or a runner with entries of its own, is 409; `vault_crosses_isolation` is 422. A runner on a set changes its list only through the set's owner (`api_runners.py::_list_admin_or_404`, 403).

## Agent credential resolve and device keys
The one route that returns secret values requires a DPoP (or AWS-role) proof from an ACTIVE device key on a runner the agent routes to or its owner owns, else 401 with members `{error: "device_proof_required", reason: "not_routed"|"no_valid_proof"}`: `apps/agents/api_credentials.py::resolve_agent_credentials` (its docstring is the contract) and `apps/agents/schemas/credentials.py`. Key listings, read logs and shared-vault logs are in the same module and `api_vaults.py`.

## Capabilities (`apps/capabilities/api.py`)
Framework tier; contract and refusals: [`capabilities.md`](capabilities.md). Leasing spends money, so lease routes need `agent.work` in the agent's workspace or the agent's own login (`_agent_to_lease_for`); a lease the caller cannot see is 404. Publishing declarations and refresh: agent owner/admins or the agent's own login. Grants and force-release: workspace `integrations` (force-release also the agent's admins). Slot data secrets are write-only and leave only sealed on the holder's lease read.
